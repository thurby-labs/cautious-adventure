Why isn’t my flag football play working?

It looked great on the whiteboard. Then on Saturday nobody was open. Most plays that fail do so for one of a handful of reasons, and almost all of them can be fixed with a route change or a better call.

The first four come with a before-and-after example. Open Grass graded both versions against Cover 1, Cover 2, Cover 3 and man; the highlighted grades are the ones the problem is about.

1Two receivers end up in the same spot

What you see: Your receivers are open on paper, but one defender is standing right between two of them.

Why it happens: When two routes finish close together, one defender can cover both.

The fix: Split them by depth. Send one deep and keep one short, so the defender in between has to pick one.

2Every route stops in front of the defense

What you see: Short throws keep getting broken up, and defenders are sitting right on top of your receivers.

Why it happens: If every route stays short, the deep defenders have nothing to guard, so they come up and crowd the short throws.

The fix: Give at least one route a job behind the defense. Even if it isn’t thrown, it pulls a defender back and opens room underneath.

3Nothing crosses against man coverage

What you see: Every receiver has a defender glued to them, and the quarterback has nowhere to go.

Why it happens: In man, each defender follows one receiver. Straight-line routes let them run right alongside.

The fix: Cross two routes close to each other. Man defenders have to run through traffic, and that’s where the space comes from.

4Your run gets caught right away

What you see: The ball carrier gets a couple of yards, then a defender pulls the flag.

Why it happens: Receivers who run short routes keep their defenders close to the ball. Against man, those defenders are right there when the run comes.

The fix: Send the receivers deep or away from the run. In man, their defenders have to go with them, and the run gets room.

5Your quarterback only looks at one receiver

What you see: Someone is wide open, but the ball goes to the receiver the play was drawn for, who’s covered.

Why it happens: Young quarterbacks lock onto the first receiver they’re told about. Against a different coverage, that receiver might be the worst option.

The fix: Give every play a read order: first look, second look, then a safe throw. The best first look changes with the coverage, so teach the quarterback which one to check against zone and which against man. Open Grass ranks the reads for each coverage, so you can see who should come first.

6It’s a good play against the wrong defense

What you see: A play that worked last week goes nowhere this week.

Why it happens: Every play has a weak spot. A play built to split two deep safeties can get an A against Cover 2 and an F against a defense with one safety in the middle.

The fix: Read the defense before you call the play, then pick the play that grades best against what you see. Here’s how to read a flag football defense in three quick looks, and example plays graded against every coverage.

Why plays work on paper and fail on Saturday

A drawing has no defense, or one that stands still. On the field, zone defenders guard areas and break on the ball, man defenders run with your receivers, and a blitzer cuts the quarterback’s time. You only find these problems when the defense moves.

Questions

Why are my receivers never open?

Usually one of three things: two receivers finish in the same spot, every route stops in front of the defense, or nothing crosses against man coverage. Spread routes by depth, send one route deep to back the defense off, and cross two routes against man.

Why does my play work in practice but not in games?

In practice the defense often knows the play or doesn’t react like a real coverage. In a game, defenders guard areas or follow receivers, and a blitzer can rush. Test the play against the coverage you expect before you run it.

Why do my running plays keep getting stopped?

Defenders near the ball are the ones who pull flags. If your receivers run short routes, their defenders stay close to the run. Send receivers deep or away from the run so their defenders go with them.

How do I know which play to call?

Count the deep defenders before the snap to figure out the coverage, then call the play that grades best against it. See how to read a defense.
