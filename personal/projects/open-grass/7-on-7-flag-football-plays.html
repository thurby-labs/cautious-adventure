What changes in 7 on 7

  • More receivers, more defenders. Most 7 on 7 leagues use a center, a quarterback and five players who can catch the ball. The defense has seven, so there’s no free player in coverage the way there can be in 5 on 5.
  • A wider field. With 40 yards sideline to sideline, the flats and the space between short defenders get bigger. Spacing your receivers matters more.
  • A rusher from 7 yards. Most leagues let defenders rush from 7 yards back. The rusher rarely pulls the flag, but they hurry the throw, so the ball needs to come out on time.
  • The center usually stays in. In many leagues the center snaps and isn’t a receiver, so the five other players carry the routes. Check your league’s rules.

What the grades tell you

  • Zone beaters and man beaters are different plays. The hitch-corner pair is an A against three zone looks and an F against man. The slants and quick outs play is the opposite.
  • Steady plays have a place. The slot crossers never grade an A, but they never grade below a B either. That’s the play to call when you can’t tell what the defense is in.
  • Alignment matters. Line the slots up too close to the ball and the inside defender can carry the corner route. A couple of yards of spacing changes the grade.
  • Your playbook needs an answer for each look. Check that at least one of your plays grades A or B against each defense you see.

Questions

How are these plays graded?

Open Grass runs each play against six 7 on 7 defenses. Defenders react the way that coverage plays, a rusher comes from 7 yards, and the grade reflects how open the best throw is and how deep it is. An A means the quarterback has a clearly open throw; an F means the defense has it covered.

Can I upload a 7 on 7 play diagram?

Yes. Upload a photo or screenshot of your play, or describe it in a sentence. Open Grass reads all seven players, including an H receiver and a running back labeled R1, RB or similar, and puts them on a 7 on 7 field.

My league’s rules are different. Does that matter?

Rules vary a lot in 7 on 7. Open Grass uses a 40-yard-wide field and a rusher from 7 yards. A center who runs a route in your diagram is treated as a receiver; one who only snaps is not. If your league rushes from closer, expect pressure to arrive sooner than the app shows.

Does Open Grass still work for 5 on 5?

Yes. Plays with five players get the 5 on 5 field and defenses automatically. See the 5 on 5 example plays.
