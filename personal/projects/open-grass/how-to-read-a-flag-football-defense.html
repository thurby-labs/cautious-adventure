How to read a flag football defense

You don’t need film or a scouting report. Before the snap, two quick looks tell you which coverage you’re facing: how many defenders are deep, and whether the rest are guarding spots on the field or following your receivers. Then call the play that beats it.

Step 1: Count the deep defenders

Look past the defenders near the line to the ones standing about 10 or more yards back. That number narrows it down right away.

1

One deep

Cover 1 or man to man. Go to step 2 to tell them apart.

2

Two deep

Cover 2. Each deep defender takes half the field.

3

Three deep

Cover 3. Each deep defender takes a third, which leaves only two defenders underneath.

Step 2: Zone or man?

In zone, defenders guard an area and react to whoever comes into it. In man, each defender follows one receiver wherever they go. Three clues give it away:

  • Where they line up. Zone defenders spread out across the field at even depths. Man defenders line up across from a receiver, wherever that receiver stands.
  • Motion. Send a receiver across the formation before the snap. If a defender runs across with them, it’s man. If the defenders just shift or call out, it’s zone.
  • Their eyes. Zone defenders watch the quarterback. Man defenders watch their receiver.

Step 3: Watch for the blitz

Any coverage can send one defender after the quarterback, which leaves one fewer in coverage. Look for a defender who creeps toward the line or stands straight across from the quarterback. Many leagues make the rusher start several yards back (often 7), so they’re easy to spot before the snap. When you see it, call a quick throw: the ball should be out before the rusher arrives.

The four coverages at a glance

What each one looks like before the snap, where it’s weak, and an example play Open Grass grades well against it.

Cover 1

One deep safety in the middle

How to spot it: One defender deep in the middle, about 12 yards back. The other four line up about 5 yards off the ball, spread across the field.

Weak spot: Only one defender deep, so two deep routes make the safety pick one.

Cover 2

Two deep safeties, one on each half

How to spot it: Two defenders deep, each splitting the field in half. The corners sit about 5 yards off the outside receivers and the linebacker stays in the middle.

Weak spot: The seam between the two safeties, and the holes between the corners and the linebacker.

See a play that beats itSpread, Seams: A

Cover 3

Three deep defenders across the field

How to spot it: Three defenders deep, one on each third of the field. Only two defenders are underneath, and they play inside.

Weak spot: The flats and short throws. Two underneath defenders can’t cover three short routes.

See a play that beats itTrips Left, Flood: A

Man to man

One deep safety, everyone else on a receiver

How to spot it: Looks like Cover 1, but each underneath defender lines up across from a receiver and follows them. Send someone in motion: if a defender runs across with them, it’s man.

Weak spot: Crossing routes and rubs. One step lost in traffic and the receiver is open.

Reading the defense on game day

  • Take the first series to look. Count the deep defenders on every snap and jot down what you see.
  • Expect them to stick with it. Many youth defenses play one coverage most of the game, so one good read can carry you.
  • Give your quarterback one job. “Count the deep defenders” is enough for young players. Tell them which side to look to for each answer.
  • Adjust at halftime. If the defense changed, put the new look on your plays and pick the ones that grade best.

Questions

How can I tell zone from man in flag football?

Send a receiver in motion before the snap. If a defender follows them across the field, it’s man. If the defenders stay put and adjust, it’s zone. Where they line up helps too: man defenders line up across from a receiver, zone defenders spread out by area.

What’s the difference between Cover 1 and man?

Before the snap they can look the same: one deep safety and four defenders underneath. In Cover 1 the underneath defenders guard areas; in man each one follows a receiver. Motion is the quickest way to tell.

How do you beat a zone defense in flag football?

Put two receivers in one defender’s area so that defender has to choose, like a corner over a flat, and throw to the one left open. Against Cover 3, attack the flats; against Cover 2, throw between the corners and the linebacker or up the seam between the safeties.

How do you beat man coverage in flag football?

Crossing routes and rubs. When routes cross close together, man defenders have to run through traffic to stay with their receiver, and that’s where the separation comes from.

Can a young quarterback really read a defense?

Yes, if you keep it to one question. Count the deep defenders before the snap, and know where to look for each answer. Coaches can do the rest of the reading from the sideline.
