Privacy Policy

Effective October 29, 2026 · Open Grass, LLC

Open Grass is a play-testing tool for youth flag football coaches at open-grass.com. This policy explains what information we collect, why, who we share it with, how long we keep it, and the choices you have. "We" and "us" mean Open Grass, LLC. The rules for using Open Grass are in our Terms of Service.

The short version

  • You can use Open Grass without an account. Your plays then stay in your own browser. You get one free AI draft; after that, AI drafting needs a free account.
  • If you sign in, we store your email (through our sign-in provider), your plays, favorites, and any play-card photos you upload, so they follow you across devices.
  • We use Google Analytics to see which features coaches use. It never receives your plays, play names, descriptions, or email. You can turn it off, and in Europe we ask before turning it on.
  • We advertise Open Grass on Google and measure whether those ads lead to sign-ups and play tests. We don't sell your information, and we don't use it to target ads at you: no ad cookies, no retargeting, no personalized ads.
  • You can download or delete your account and its data at any time from Your data at the bottom of the app.

1. Information we collect

If you use Open Grass without an account

Plays you save, your favorites, and the last play you opened are kept in your browser's local storage on your device. We don't receive them. If you rate a suggested fix, see Fix ratings below. Clearing your browser data, or using Your data → Clear Open Grass data from this browser, deletes them.

If you create an account

  • Account details. Sign-in is handled by our provider, Clerk. Clerk stores your email address and, if you sign in with Google, the name and profile picture Google shares. Clerk also keeps sign-in records (such as time, IP address and browser) to keep accounts secure. We receive a random account ID from Clerk, not your password.
  • Your plays and favorites. The plays you save, their names, your list of favorites, and which sample plays you hid, stored with your account ID.
  • Play-card photos. If you create a play from a photo or screenshot while signed in, we keep the image so you can view the original later. Only you can access it. If no saved play uses a photo, we delete it after 7 days. We only keep photos that pass the check described under AI drafting.
  • PDF playbooks. If you upload a playbook as a PDF while signed in, the PDF itself stays on your device. The app draws each page you pick as an image, and only those page images are sent, just like a photo. We keep a page image only when plays were found on it, so you can view the original page later; it's handled and deleted like a play-card photo. Pages that are skipped (no plays on them, or a photo of people) are never stored.

When you use AI drafting

Without an account you get one free AI draft; after that, AI drafting needs a free account. When you describe a play in words, upload a diagram, or upload pages of a PDF playbook, that text or image is sent to our server and on to Anthropic, which provides the AI model that draws the play. Before an image leaves your device, the app redraws it, which removes its location and camera details (EXIF data) and shrinks it. The same AI model also checks every request. We turn down inappropriate descriptions. An image is rejected if it shows people, has inappropriate content, or isn't a play diagram. In that case no play is drawn, we never store the image, and we record only that it was rejected. A PDF page with no plays or with people on it is skipped the same way. For each request we also record the time, whether it was text or an image, whether it worked, how much AI processing it used, your account ID, and a salted, one-way hash of your IP address. We use these to enforce daily limits, prevent abuse, and manage costs. If an account has three requests turned down as inappropriate within 30 days, we turn off AI drafting for that account; email us if you think that was a mistake. Our Terms of Service list what isn't allowed. We do not store your IP address itself. After 90 days we remove the hashed IP and account ID from these records. Daily usage counters are deleted after 30 days.

Fix ratings

After you try one of the app's suggested route fixes, you can rate it 👍 or 👎 and, after a 👎, pick a reason from a short list. We store each rating on our server so we can find fixes that coaches keep turning down and improve them. A rating includes only the app's own settings and terms: the format, defense and rusher settings, which position the fix was for, the route the app suggested, the grades before and after, and the reason you picked. It never includes your play, your play's name, or your own route names (for the app's sample plays, it includes which sample play it was). With it we store your account ID if you're signed in, or a salted, one-way hash of your IP address if you're not, so one person counts once and can't flood the tally; at most 50 ratings a day are accepted from one IP address. After 90 days we remove the account ID and hashed IP, and keep only the anonymous rating. Turning off usage statistics (section 6), or a Global Privacy Control signal from your browser, stops ratings from being sent; your 👍 or 👎 is then remembered only in your browser.

Feedback you send us

During the beta, the Beta button and Send feedback link open a short form. We store what you write, the topic you pick, and, unless you uncheck "Include what's on screen," the app's own settings at that moment: the format, defense, rusher settings and grade (and which sample play, for the app's sample plays; never your own play or its name). We also note whether you're on a phone, tablet or computer. If you're signed in, the note is stored with your account ID, and we reply to your account email only if you check the box asking for a reply. If you're not signed in, you can add an email address for a reply; otherwise we store a salted, one-way hash of your IP address to stop spam (at most 10 notes a day from one IP address). Feedback is sent even when usage statistics are off, because you chose to send it; Google Analytics only learns that a note was sent and its topic, never what you wrote. After 90 days we remove the account ID, email address and hashed IP, and we delete the note after one year. To make sure we see new notes quickly, an AI tool from Anthropic copies each new note, with its details and the email for a reply, into an email to our own inbox (Gmail). Please don't put personal details in a note that you don't want us to keep.

Analytics

We use Google Analytics to understand how the app is used, for example which defenses coaches test their plays against, whether AI drafting and PDF imports work, and whether share links get opened. Google Analytics uses cookies (_ga, _ga_*) and collects information such as pages visited, the features you use, your browser and device type, and approximate location (city level, derived from your IP address by Google). In Europe it only runs after you allow it (see Visitors in Europe).

  • What the app reports is about the app's own features: buttons and settings you use (such as the defense, rusher, speed and grade), whether a play was a sample play (and which one) or one of your own (only whether it's a pass or a run), suggested fixes you try or rate (in the app's own route terms), counts such as how many PDF pages you picked, and a rough count of how many plays you've saved (for example "1–5").
  • We deliberately never send your plays, play names, play descriptions, file names, or email address to Google Analytics, and we remove the part of a link that contains a shared play before the page address is recorded.
  • If you're signed in, we send your random account ID so visits from your different devices count as one coach. We never send your email or name.
  • Google signals and ad personalization are turned off. Analytics data is kept for 14 months.
  • See how Google uses information from sites that use its services.

Advertising on Google

We advertise Open Grass with Google Ads. Our Google Ads account is linked to the same Google tag as Analytics, which lets us see whether our ads work:

  • When a page loads, Google Ads receives a signal that the page was viewed, without setting any advertising cookies. Like any web request, it includes your IP address and browser information. It never includes the part of a link that holds a shared play.
  • If you click one of our ads and then create an account, or run one of your own plays against a defense, Google Ads learns that a sign-up or a play test happened, so we can count what our ads bring in. It isn't told your email, name, plays, or anything else you put in Open Grass.
  • We don't use remarketing or retargeting (following you around the web with our ads), we don't show personalized ads or build ad audiences, and we don't give Google your email or plays for advertising.
  • Turning off analytics (section 6), or a Global Privacy Control signal from your browser, stops these signals too.

Share links

When you share a play you created or changed, the entire play is encoded inside the link itself. We don't store it on a server. Anyone who has the link can open the play, so share it only with people you want to see it.

Information your browser sends automatically

Like any website, the services that deliver Open Grass receive your IP address and basic browser information: our host (GitHub Pages) when you load a page, and our server provider (Cloudflare) when you use AI drafting or an account, rate a fix, or send feedback.

2. How we use information

  • To run the app: save and sync your plays, draw plays with AI, and show your photos back to you.
  • To keep it working and affordable: rate limits, daily AI limits, abuse prevention, and cost tracking.
  • To improve it: learning which features coaches use (through analytics) and which suggested fixes they rate down.
  • To see whether our ads on Google lead to sign-ups and play tests, as described under Advertising on Google.
  • To read your feedback, fix what you report, and reply when you ask us to.
  • To respond to you when you contact us.
  • To send you account emails, like a welcome note when you create an account and notice of important changes to Open Grass or these policies. If we ever send product news, you can opt out anytime by replying to any of our emails.

We don't sell personal information, and we don't share it for cross-context behavioral advertising. We don't use your plays or photos to train AI models.

6. Your choices and rights

  • Turn off analytics. In the app, open Your data (bottom of the page) and uncheck "Share usage statistics." This also stops the Google Ads signals and stops fix ratings from being sent. If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, we turn analytics and these ad signals off automatically. You can also use Google's Analytics opt-out browser add-on.
  • Download your data. If you're signed in, choose Your data → Download my account data for a file with your plays, favorites, photo list, AI draft history, fix ratings and feedback notes. Export favorites also saves your favorite plays as a file you can import elsewhere.
  • Correct your data. Edit or delete plays in the app. Update your email or name from the account menu (your profile picture, top right).
  • Delete your data. Delete any play you've saved, or choose Your data → Delete my account to permanently delete your account, plays, favorites, hidden sample plays, photos and feedback notes; your fix ratings are kept only as anonymous ratings, with your account ID removed. Not signed in? Use Clear Open Grass data from this browser.
  • Ask us. You can also email us to access, correct or delete your information, including analytics data tied to your account. We'll respond within 30 days and may need to confirm the request comes from you.

Depending on where you live (for example California, or other U.S. states with privacy laws), you may have additional rights, such as to know what we collect, to have it deleted or corrected, and not to be treated differently for exercising these rights. We honor these rights for all users, wherever they live.

Visitors in Europe (EU, EEA, UK and Switzerland)

If you're in Europe, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) or the UK and Swiss equivalents apply. Open Grass, LLC is the controller of your information; contact details are in section 11.

  • We ask before analytics. If your device's time zone is in Europe, the app doesn't load Google Analytics or the Google Ads signals, and doesn't send fix ratings, until you tap Allow on the banner. No thanks keeps them off, and the banner won't ask again. You can change your mind anytime under Your data; turning usage statistics off also deletes the analytics cookies from your browser. Our fonts are served from our own site, so loading a page doesn't contact Google.
  • Why we're allowed to use your information (legal bases):
    • To provide what you ask for (your account, saving and syncing plays, photos, AI drafting, account emails): to perform our agreement with you (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
    • To keep Open Grass safe and affordable (rate limits, daily AI limits, content checks, salted IP hashes, sign-in security records) and to read and answer your feedback: our legitimate interest in running a secure, sustainable service (Art. 6(1)(f)).
    • Usage statistics, Google Ads measurement and fix ratings: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a)), which you can withdraw at any time without affecting what happened before.
    • Where the law requires us to keep or disclose something: our legal obligations (Art. 6(1)(c)).
  • Your rights. You can ask to access, correct or delete your information, to restrict or object to how we use it (including anything based on legitimate interest), and to receive it in a portable format. Most of this is self-serve under Your data; otherwise email us and we'll answer within one month.
  • Automated decisions. We don't make decisions with legal or similarly significant effects about you by automated means alone. If the automatic content check turns off AI drafting for your account, email us and a person will review it.
  • Transfers outside Europe. Open Grass is run from the United States, so your information is processed there. We rely on our providers' certification under the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework (and its UK and Swiss extensions) where they have one, and otherwise on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses in their data processing terms.
  • Complaints. You have the right to complain to the data protection authority where you live or work (in Germany, your state's data protection authority; in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office). The EU authorities are listed on the European Data Protection Board's site. We'd appreciate the chance to fix things first, so please email us.

7. Children's privacy

Open Grass is built for coaches, parents and other adults. It isn't directed to children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has given us personal information, contact us and we'll delete it. Coaches: please don't put players' full names or other personal details about children into play names, descriptions or photos.

8. Security

Connections to Open Grass are encrypted (HTTPS). Account data is only available to the signed-in account it belongs to, photos are stored privately, and IP addresses are stored only as salted hashes. No system is perfectly secure, but we work to protect your information and will notify you as required by law if a breach affects it.

9. Where information is processed

Open Grass is operated from the United States. Our providers may process information in the United States and other countries where they operate. For visitors in Europe, Visitors in Europe explains how these transfers are protected.

10. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update the effective date above. If the changes are significant, we'll also tell you in the app.
