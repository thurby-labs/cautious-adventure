What the grades tell you

  • Every play has a weak spot. Even the flood, an A against all three zones, drops to a C against man. Knowing the weak spot is what lets you call the right play.
  • Specialists are fine. The seams play is an F against one deep safety, but an A against Cover 2. Keep it for the teams that play Cover 2.
  • Small changes matter. One route change turns the corner-flat play from a D to a B against man.
  • Your playbook needs an answer for each look. Check that at least one of your plays grades A or B against each of Cover 1, Cover 2, Cover 3 and man.

Questions

How are these plays graded?

Open Grass runs each play against Cover 1, Cover 2, Cover 3 and man to man. Defenders react the way that coverage plays, and the grade reflects how open the best throw is, or how far the ball carrier gets on a run. An A means the quarterback has a clearly open throw, or the runner gets well past the line; an F means the defense has it covered.

What makes a good flag football play for kids?

Short throws and simple reads. Young quarterbacks are most accurate inside 10 yards, so plays built on flats, slants and hitches, with one clear read, work best.
